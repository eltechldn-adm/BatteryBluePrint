This comparison reviews four residential storage systems frequently installed in the US and UK markets. It evaluates measurable performance specifications, warranty duration, and system architecture.

We do not accept sponsorship. This comparison is based exclusively on **Manufacturer Datasheet Specifications**, **Published Warranty Terms**, and **Documented System Architecture**.

---

## Methodology and Comparison Criteria

BatteryBlueprint compares residential energy storage systems using manufacturer-reported technical specifications. **We have not physically bench-tested these products.** Our comparison relies on published data sheets and warranty documents to evaluate:

- **Continuous Output (kW):** The sustained AC power the battery can provide according to the manufacturer.
- **Peak Output (kW):** The surge AC power available for starting heavy loads.
- **Published Energy / Capacity:** The actual energy available for discharge as published.
- **Warranty Duration:** The manufacturer's stated term and cycle or throughput limit.

This page contains no BatteryBlueprint-derived metrics; all values are manufacturer-reported. Pricing is excluded from this technical comparison because manufacturer-direct MSRPs are rarely published or fixed across regions. For financial return estimates, see our [Solar Battery Payback Reality](/payback-reality) or use the [battery calculator](/calculator).

*Note: Manufacturers use different capacity terminology. Values below preserve the terminology used in the cited manufacturer documentation and may not represent an identical measurement basis.*

## 1. Tesla Powerwall 3

A fully integrated AC-coupled system with a built-in hybrid solar inverter.

*   **Chemistry:** Lithium-ion.
*   **Published Energy / Capacity:** 13.5 kWh nominal battery energy.
*   **Manufacturer Continuous Power Output:** 11.5 kW.
*   **Architecture:** Built-in Hybrid Solar Inverter.
*   **Regional Specification Basis:** North American specification.

**Key Technical Attributes:**
*   11.5 kW continuous output for heavy load starting.
*   Manufacturer publishes a 185 A LRA (Locked Rotor Amps) load start capability.
*   Integrated solar inverter reduces balance-of-system hardware on new installations.

## Key Specification Definitions

### 1. Continuous Power Output (kW)
Determines instantaneous load capability. 
*   **3-5 kW:** Sufficient for standard 120V/230V household loads (refrigerators, lighting, electronics).
*   **10 kW+:** Required for starting and running heavy 240V loads like central air conditioning without grid support.

### 2. Published Energy / Capacity
Determines duration of backup or arbitrage operation.

### 3. Chemistry
*   **LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate):** Features excellent thermal stability. 
*   **Lithium-ion:** A broader category of chemistries; check manufacturer documentation for specific safety ratings and thermal management systems.

## Engineering Reality

Battery brand comparisons on specification sheets represent controlled-condition best-case performance. Several factors cause real-world systems to diverge from the datasheet.

**Efficiency metrics use different baselines.** Manufacturers may quote DC round-trip efficiency (which excludes inverter losses) or AC round-trip efficiency (which includes them). These cannot be directly compared.

**Software ecosystems.** Certain manufacturers restrict API access, requiring users to rely entirely on their proprietary cloud applications. If you intend to use a third-party aggregator, confirm that your battery's API is officially documented and accessible before purchasing.

**Warranty depth matters as much as warranty length.** FranklinWH and Enphase both offer 15-year warranties. However, warranty coverage terms differ significantly regarding capacity retention guarantees, throughput limits (e.g., 60 MWh), and labor reimbursement. Read the official warranty document before making a final comparison.
