If you live in California, the state has allocated funds to assist qualifying low-income homeowners installing paired solar-plus-storage through the **Self-Generation Incentive Program (SGIP)**.

For eligible customers, SGIP can significantly reduce the cost of an energy storage system.

**Update 2026:** The active residential budget category is the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget. Availability varies by programme administrator. Check current [SGIP Program Metrics](https://www.selfgenca.com/home/program_metrics) before relying on an incentive.

---

## The Current 2026 Programme: Residential Solar and Storage Equity

The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) has prioritized remaining SGIP funding for low-income residents installing battery storage.

### Residential Solar and Storage Equity Budget
*   **Rebate Amount**: Up to **$1,100 per kWh** of storage capacity (subject to cost caps and limitations based on solar capacity).
*   **Who Qualifies?**: This category is exclusively for qualifying low-income customers who are installing paired solar-plus-storage systems. Eligibility depends on strict income thresholds and documentation verifying low-income status as defined by CPUC guidelines.
*   **Status**: This is the active residential budget. Funding is subject to availability by territory, and applications may be placed on waitlists.

*(Source: CPUC SGIP Program Metrics and Guidelines)*

## Historical SGIP Categories

As of 2026, the following categories are no longer active application pathways:

*   **Equity Resiliency and Small Residential Storage**: The CPUC identified these funds as available only through 2025. They are not an active funding stream for new 2026 applications.

## The Application Process

SGIP is a multi-step process defined by the CPUC SGIP Handbook.

### Phase 1: Reservation Request
Your Developer submits the application.
*   **Result**: You may receive a "Conditional Reservation Letter." This reserves funding based on your application details, provided all subsequent milestones and conditions are met.

### Phase 2: Proof of Project Milestone (PPM)
*   You must prove the project is moving forward.
*   **Documents**: Often requires a signed building permit or equipment purchase order, per the Program Administrator's timeline.

### Phase 3: Incentive Claim Form (ICF)
*   The system is installed, inspected, and interconnected.
*   **Documents**: Final Building Permit, monitoring confirmation, and interconnection approval.

### Phase 4: Payment Review
*   The Program Administrator (PA) reviews the final claim.
*   **Result**: If approved, payment is issued. Payment timelines vary significantly based on administrator volume.

---

## Glossary of Terms

*   **Program Administrator (PA)**: The entity running SGIP in your area. This includes Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E), Southern California Edison (SCE), Southern California Gas Company (SoCalGas), Center for Sustainable Energy (CSE) which administers for SDG&E, and the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP).
*   **Host Customer**: You (the homeowner).
*   **Developer**: The entity submitting the paperwork. You can search the approved developer list on the SGIP portal, but note that CPUC approval of a developer for SGIP purposes does not constitute an endorsement.
*   **PPM (Proof of Project Milestone)**: The deadline to show you have a permit or purchase order.
*   **ICF (Incentive Claim Form)**: The final paperwork to claim payment.

## The History of SGIP

SGIP started in 2001. It was originally designed to encourage businesses to install their own generation during the energy crisis. The program continues to evolve, with current budget categories prioritizing low-income residents and equity communities for paired solar-plus-storage systems.

## Engineering Reality

The SGIP programme's structure is well-documented, but several operational features dictate how the programme functions today.

**SGIP rebates are subject to cost caps and technical limits.** The current Residential Solar and Storage Equity rebate of up to $1,100/kWh is strictly capped based on project costs and technical limits tied to the paired solar system's capacity. The final approved rebate may be lower than the maximum theoretical value if the installation costs are lower than the cap or if the battery size exceeds the eligible ratio to the solar array.

## Engineering Recommendation

SGIP provides meaningful financial support for eligible California low-income residents installing paired solar and storage.

**Priority actions for California homeowners who may be eligible:**
1. Confirm income-eligibility for the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget.
2. Confirm active budget availability on the CPUC's SGIP portal for your specific Program Administrator (PG&E, SCE, SoCalGas, CSE, or LADWP) before signing any contract.
3. Review the current SGIP Handbook to ensure your proposed system meets all technical eligibility criteria.

**Stacking SGIP with other incentives:**
- For eligible 2025 filers with carry-forward credits, utility rebates may reduce the federal ITC basis. Consult a tax professional.
