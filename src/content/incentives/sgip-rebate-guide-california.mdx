If you live in California, the state has allocated funds to assist qualifying low-income homeowners installing paired solar-plus-storage through the **Self-Generation Incentive Program (SGIP)**.

For eligible customers, SGIP can significantly reduce the cost of an energy storage system.

**August 2026 status:** The active residential budget category is the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget. Availability varies by programme administrator. Some budgets are exhausted or waitlisted. Being placed on a waitlist does not guarantee funding and depends on future CPUC appropriations. Check current [SGIP Program Metrics](https://www.selfgenca.com/home/program_metrics) and waitlists before relying on an incentive.

---

## The Current 2026 Programme: Residential Solar and Storage Equity

The California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) has prioritized remaining SGIP funding for low-income residents installing battery storage. 

### Residential Solar and Storage Equity Budget
*   **Rebate Amount**: Up to **$1,100 per kWh** of storage capacity (subject to cost caps and limitations based on solar capacity).
*   **Who Qualifies?**: This category is exclusively for qualifying low-income customers who are installing paired solar-plus-storage systems. Eligibility depends on strict income thresholds and documentation verifying low-income status as defined by CPUC guidelines.
*   **Status**: This is the current active residential budget. Funding is subject to availability by territory, and applications may be placed on waitlists.

*(Source: CPUC SGIP Program Metrics and Guidelines)*

## Historical SGIP Categories

As of 2026, the following categories are no longer active application pathways, but may still be processing waitlisted projects from prior years:

*   **Equity Resiliency and Small Residential Storage**: The CPUC identified these funds as available only through 2025. They are not an active funding stream for new 2026 applications unless you are on an existing waitlist that receives new appropriations. (Historically, Equity Resiliency provided funding for customers in High Fire Threat Districts with a Medical Baseline allowance or prior power shutoff events).
*   **General Market ($150/kWh)**: Funding for this tier is exhausted in most residential territories. It is not a reliable source of funding for new 2026 installations.

## The Application Process

SGIP is a multi-step process.

### Phase 1: Reservation Request
Your installer (the "Developer") submits the application.
*   **Result**: You receive a "Confirmed Reservation Letter." If you are relying on the incentive, do not install before receiving confirmation.

### Phase 2: Proof of Project Milestone (PPM)
*   You must prove the project is moving forward.
*   **Documents**: Often requires a signed building permit or equipment purchase order, per the PA's timeline.

### Phase 3: Incentive Claim Form (ICF)
*   The system is installed, inspected, and interconnected.
*   **Documents**: Final Building Permit, monitoring confirmation, and interconnection approval.

### Phase 4: Payment Review
*   The Program Administrator (PA) reviews the final claim.
*   **Result**: If approved, payment is issued. Payment timelines vary significantly based on administrator volume.

---

## Glossary of Terms

*   **Program Administrator (PA)**: The entity running SGIP in your area. This includes Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E), Southern California Edison (SCE), Southern California Gas Company (SoCalGas), Center for Sustainable Energy (CSE) which administers for SDG&E, and the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP).
*   **Host Customer**: You (the homeowner).
*   **Developer**: The installer submitting the paperwork.
*   **PPM (Proof of Project Milestone)**: The deadline to show you have a permit.
*   **ICF (Incentive Claim Form)**: The final paperwork to get paid.

## The History of SGIP

SGIP started in 2001, long before lithium batteries. It was originally designed to encourage businesses to install their own generation during the energy crisis. The program continues to evolve, with current budget categories prioritizing low-income residents and equity communities for paired solar-plus-storage systems.

## Engineering Reality

The SGIP programme's structure is well-documented, but several operational features dictate how the programme functions today.

**SGIP rebates are subject to cost caps and technical limits.** The current Residential Solar and Storage Equity rebate of up to $1,100/kWh is not a blank check. It is strictly capped based on project costs and technical limits tied to the paired solar system's capacity. The final approved rebate may be lower than the maximum theoretical value if the installation costs are lower than the cap or if the battery size exceeds the eligible ratio to the solar array.

**Incentive budget exhaustion creates funding cliff risks.** SGIP funding is structured in tranches. As each step's funding is exhausted, the programme moves to waitlists. Confirming active budget availability with your specific Program Administrator on the day of application — not the day of the quote — is the operationally correct approach.

---

## When This Approach Breaks Down

SGIP requires explicit attention to programme rules in several circumstances.

**Aggregated or multi-unit residential properties.** SGIP applications for individual units within a multi-unit residential building (apartment, condominium) must navigate the utility's interconnection rules for shared meters and submetering. Where the building has a master meter, individual units may not be eligible for individual customer rebates under the SGIP residential programme.

**Systems installed by non-SGIP-registered contractors.** SGIP applications must be submitted by an SGIP-registered contractor. Not all licensed contractors are SGIP-registered. An installation by a non-SGIP-registered contractor, even if the work is technically compliant, cannot generate an SGIP rebate application. Confirming that the installer holds current SGIP registration before signing a contract is a prerequisite for rebate eligibility.

## Engineering Recommendation

SGIP provides meaningful financial support for eligible California low-income residents installing paired solar and storage.

**Priority actions for California homeowners who may be eligible:**
1. Confirm income-eligibility for the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget.
2. Confirm active budget availability on the CPUC's SGIP portal for your specific Program Administrator (PG&E, SCE, SoCalGas, CSE, or LADWP) before signing any contract.
3. Confirm the installer's SGIP registration status.

**Stacking SGIP with other incentives:**
- For eligible 2025 filers with carry-forward credits, utility rebates may reduce the federal ITC basis. Consult a tax professional.
- SGIP does not affect California's existing property tax exclusion for active solar energy systems.
