**Critical update — September 2026:** The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (the 30% battery and solar tax credit) was terminated for property placed in service after 31 December 2025. This page explains the current status, what options remain, and what 2025 filers with carry-forward credits need to know.

*Primary source: IRS guidance on Section 25D; 26 U.S. Code § 25D; H.R. 1 ("One Big Beautiful Bill"), signed July 2025. Reviewed September 2026.*

## What Happened to the 30% Federal ITC?

The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 had legislated the residential Section 25D credit at 30% through 2032. That schedule was overridden by H.R. 1, signed into law in July 2025 ("One Big Beautiful Bill"), which terminated the residential credit for any qualified clean energy property **placed in service after 31 December 2025**.

There is **no phase-down** — the credit went from 30% to 0% for 2026 installations without the 26%/22% step-down schedule that was previously legislated.

**For homeowners installing in 2026 or later:** No federal residential tax credit is available under Section 25D.

**For homeowners who installed in 2025 or earlier:** If you have unused Section 25D credit from an eligible installation completed on or before 31 December 2025, you can still carry that credit forward to offset federal tax liability in future tax years. Note that Section 25D explicitly applies only to battery storage technology with a capacity of **3 kilowatt-hours or greater** (source: 26 U.S. Code § 25D(a)(6)).

## Section 25D vs Section 48 (MACRS)

It is critical not to confuse the **residential** Section 25D credit with **commercial** tax treatments under Section 48 or Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System (MACRS) depreciation.

Section 25D was the credit available directly to homeowners purchasing systems for their primary or secondary residences. Section 48 and MACRS apply to commercial and third-party-owned systems. The termination of Section 25D does not automatically terminate Section 48 commercial credits, but residential homeowners purchasing their own systems cannot claim Section 48 or MACRS.

### Section 48E — Commercial and Third-Party Ownership

The Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit may be available to qualifying commercial and third-party owners. This means:
- If you **lease** a battery or enter a **PPA (power purchase agreement)**, the financing company — not you — may claim Section 48E and MACRS on the system.
- You do not receive a direct tax credit in this scenario, but you may benefit indirectly through lower monthly lease payments if the provider passes through some of the credit value.
- Confirm with any third-party provider whether and how they pass through this benefit.

## State-Level Incentives

Several states maintain active battery storage incentive programmes that do not depend on the federal Section 25D credit:

**California — SGIP (Self-Generation Incentive Program):**
Availability varies by SGIP budget category and programme administrator. The active Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget is strictly for qualifying low-income customers. Standard General Market budgets are mostly exhausted or waitlisted. Check current [SGIP Program Metrics](https://www.selfgenca.com/home/program_metrics).

**New York — NY-Sun and VDER:**
New York offers the NY-Sun Storage Incentive (administered by NYSERDA, with amounts varying by utility territory) and ongoing compensation through the Value of Distributed Energy Resources (VDER) tariff. Check [nyserda.ny.gov](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov) for current status.

**Massachusetts — SMART Programme:**
Massachusetts offers the Solar Massachusetts Renewable Target (SMART) programme, which provides a performance-based incentive for solar+storage. Check with your utility (Eversource or National Grid) for current capacity blocks.

**Hawaii — State Tax Credit:**
Hawaii's state 35% tax credit (capped at $5,000) is a state-level instrument and is not affected by the federal Section 25D termination. Confirm current status at the Hawaii Department of Taxation.

**DSIRE Database:** The most comprehensive source for current state, utility, and local incentive availability is [dsireusa.org](https://www.dsireusa.org/).

## For 2025 Filers: Claiming a Carry-Forward Credit

If your battery system was placed in service on or before 31 December 2025, you may have an eligible Section 25D credit. Under IRS rules, unused Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) from an eligible prior year may still be carried forward to the succeeding tax year.

**Carry-forward mechanics:**
- **Specific Caveat:** The residential Section 25D credit is a **non-refundable** credit. This means it can only offset federal income tax liability to zero; it will not generate a tax refund for amounts exceeding your liability.
- If your 2025 credit exceeded your 2025 federal tax liability, the unused portion carries forward to 2026.
- The carry-forward can continue year-to-year until the credit is fully used.

**Documentation to retain (IRS audit window: 7 years):**
- Final itemised installation invoice showing all costs
- Proof of installation date (permit sign-off, utility interconnection date, or commissioning documentation)
- IRS Form 5695 from the filing year
- Copy of your tax return showing credit applied

**Common 2025 carry-forward errors:**
1. **Capacity threshold:** Claiming the credit for a battery with a capacity under 3 kWh.
2. **ITC basis overclaim:** Only costs directly attributable to the eligible battery system qualify. Main panel upgrades not required by the battery installation are typically excluded.
3. **Failure to reduce basis by state rebates:** Utility and state rebates that reduced your installation cost must be subtracted from the ITC basis before calculating the 30% credit.
4. **Assuming full Year 1 utilisation:** If your 2025 federal tax liability was below the credit value, only the amount equal to your tax liability was used in 2025. The remainder carries forward.

Consult a qualified tax professional before filing your 2025 or 2026 carry-forward return.

## What Does This Mean for Battery Purchase Economics in 2026?

Without the 30% federal credit, the economics of residential battery storage in the US change materially for 2026 buyers.

**Current example (2026 purchase, no federal credit):**
- Gross installed cost: $14,000
- No federal residential credit available
- Net cost: **$14,000**
- Payback at $1,800/year savings: approximately 7.8 years

In high-rate states (California, New York, Massachusetts) with strong state incentives and time-of-use tariff arbitrage, battery storage remains financially viable. In low-rate flat-tariff states without state-level support, the financial case is significantly weaker without the federal credit.

## How to Evaluate Battery Economics Without the Federal Credit

For homeowners considering battery storage in 2026, the financial analysis requires a more careful regional approach now that the federal credit is unavailable.

**Step 1: Confirm state and utility incentives**
Use the [DSIRE database](https://www.dsireusa.org/) to check every state, utility, and local incentive available at your address.

**Step 2: Calculate your actual TOU savings**
Battery payback is determined primarily by the spread between off-peak and peak electricity rates on your tariff. The arbitrage value is often the dominant return driver.

**Step 3: Value backup honestly**
Without the federal credit reducing upfront cost, backup power capability becomes a more significant part of the total value proposition.

**Step 4: Compare with rising hardware efficiency**
Battery hardware costs have fallen significantly, partially compensating for the absent federal credit.

**Step 5: Check lease/PPA terms for Section 48E pass-through**
Third-party ownership models may pass through some value from the Section 48E commercial credit. You do not directly receive the Section 48E credit when a third party owns the system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I claim the federal ITC if I install a battery in 2026?**
No. The Section 25D residential credit does not apply to systems placed in service after 31 December 2025.

**Does the credit apply if I signed a contract in 2025 but installed in 2026?**
The determining date is when the system is placed in service (fully installed and operational), not when the contract was signed or paid. If installation was completed in January 2026 or later, the credit does not apply.

**Can I claim the credit on a battery added to an existing solar system in 2026?**
No — the Section 25D credit applies to property placed in service before 1 January 2026.

**What if I lease my battery from a provider?**
The leasing company may be eligible for the Section 48E commercial credit. You do not directly receive a tax credit. Confirm all savings and terms with your specific provider.
