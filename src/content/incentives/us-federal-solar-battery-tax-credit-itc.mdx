**Critical update — September 2026:** The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (the 30% battery and solar tax credit) was terminated for property placed in service after 31 December 2025. This page explains the current status, what options remain, and what 2025 filers with carry-forward credits need to know.

*Primary source: IRS guidance on Section 25D; 26 U.S. Code § 25D. Reviewed September 2026.*

## What Happened to the 30% Federal ITC?

The residential [Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit) was terminated for any qualified clean energy property **placed in service after 31 December 2025**.

There is **no phase-down** — the credit went from 30% to 0% for 2026 installations.

**For homeowners installing in 2026 or later:** No federal residential tax credit is available under Section 25D.

**For homeowners who installed in 2025 or earlier:** If you have unused Section 25D credit from an eligible installation completed on or before 31 December 2025, you can still carry that credit forward to offset federal tax liability in future tax years. Note that Section 25D explicitly applies only to battery storage technology with a capacity of **3 kilowatt-hours or greater** (source: 26 U.S. Code § 25D(a)(6)).

## Section 25D vs Section 48E

It is critical not to confuse the **residential** Section 25D credit with **commercial** tax treatments under the [Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/clean-electricity-investment-credit).

Section 25D was the credit available directly to homeowners purchasing systems for their primary or secondary residences. Section 48E applies to commercial and third-party-owned systems. The termination of Section 25D does not automatically terminate Section 48E commercial credits, but Section 48E is not a homeowner replacement for Section 25D.

### Section 48E — Commercial and Third-Party Ownership

The Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit generally has a 6% base rate and may reach 30% when applicable statutory requirements or exceptions are satisfied. This means:
- If you **lease** a battery or enter a **PPA (power purchase agreement)**, the third-party owner might be able to claim Section 48E on the system if they meet all eligibility requirements.
- You do not receive a direct tax credit in this scenario. Whether any benefit is passed through to you via lower lease payments is a contractual matter with your specific provider.

## For 2025 Filers: Claiming a Carry-Forward Credit

If your battery system was placed in service on or before 31 December 2025, you may have an eligible Section 25D credit. Under IRS rules, unused Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D) from an eligible prior year may still be carried forward to the succeeding tax year.

**Carry-forward mechanics:**
- **Specific Caveat:** The residential Section 25D credit is a **non-refundable** credit. This means it can only offset federal income tax liability to zero; it will not generate a tax refund for amounts exceeding your liability.
- If your 2025 credit exceeded your 2025 federal tax liability, the unused portion carries forward to 2026.
- The carry-forward can continue year-to-year until the credit is fully used.

**Documentation to retain:**
- Keep all documentation related to the installation, including invoices, permit sign-offs, and interconnection dates. Consult IRS guidance on recordkeeping for your specific situation.

**Common 2025 carry-forward error:**
- **Capacity threshold:** Claiming the credit for a battery with a capacity under 3 kWh.

Consult IRS guidance and a qualified tax professional to determine your eligible cost basis and before filing your 2025 or 2026 carry-forward return.

For homeowners considering battery storage in 2026, the financial analysis requires checking regional and utility-specific programmes now that the federal credit is unavailable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I claim the federal ITC if I install a battery in 2026?**
No. The Section 25D residential credit does not apply to systems placed in service after 31 December 2025.

**Does the credit apply if I signed a contract in 2025 but installed in 2026?**
The determining date is when the system is placed in service (fully installed and operational), not when the contract was signed or paid. If installation was completed in January 2026 or later, the credit does not apply.

**Can I claim the credit on a battery added to an existing solar system in 2026?**
No — the Section 25D credit applies to property placed in service before 1 January 2026.

**What if I lease my battery from a provider?**
The leasing company may be eligible for the Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit if they satisfy all statutory requirements. You do not directly receive a tax credit. Confirm all terms with your specific provider.
