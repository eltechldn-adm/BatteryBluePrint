The sticker price of a home battery system—often $12,000 to $15,000 or more—is significant, but state and utility incentives can reduce the final cost.

**Critical Update (September 2026):** The federal Section 25D residential ITC was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. State rebates, utility incentives, and Virtual Power Plant (VPP) payments remain available and are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

This guide aggregates solar battery incentives available across the United States. We highlight state-level programs, performance payments, and how to evaluate their impact.

---

## Part 1: The Foundation (Post-Federal ITC)

The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (30% of the total installed cost) was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. **No federal residential ITC is available for new 2026 battery purchases.** State rebates, utility programmes, and VPP payments are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

If you installed before 1 January 2026 and have unused credit, you may carry that forward. Review the [US Federal ITC status guide](/incentives/us-federal-solar-battery-tax-credit-itc) for carry-forward rules.

### Direct Pay (For Non-Profits)
If you are a church, school, or non-profit, you don’t pay taxes, so a "tax credit" is useless. The Inflation Reduction Act introduced "Direct Pay" for commercial and non-profit entities. The IRS will write a check for the applicable Section 48/48E commercial credit. Note: Direct Pay does not apply to the expired Section 25D residential homeowner credit.

## Part 2: States with Active Incentive Programs

Several states offer upfront cash rebates and/or ongoing performance payments for energy storage.

### 1. California (SGIP)
*   **Program**: [Self-Generation Incentive Program (CPUC)](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip)
*   **Incentive**: Up to **$1,100/kWh** under the current Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, subject to available funding and cost caps.
*   **Status**: The CPUC has prioritized remaining residential SGIP funding exclusively for the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, which targets qualifying low-income customers installing paired solar-plus-storage. 
*   **Historical Note**: Previous broader categories like Equity Resiliency, Small Residential Storage, and General Market are exhausted in most territories or identified by the CPUC as available only through 2025 (serving only legacy waitlisted applications).

### 2. New York (NY-Sun & VDER)
New York offers a stacked incentive structure combining an upfront rebate with ongoing compensation.
*   **NY-Sun Storage Incentive**: An upfront rebate administered by NYSERDA. Amounts vary dynamically by utility territory block (e.g., $250–$400/kWh). Funding blocks deplete over time.
*   **VDER (Value of Distributed Energy Resources)**: An ongoing compensation mechanism (tariff) rather than an upfront rebate. VDER compensates battery owners for exporting power to the grid, particularly during high-value peak periods.
*   **Status**: Active. Check [NYSERDA](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov) for current NY-Sun block capacity and utility-specific VDER rates.

### 3. Massachusetts (SMART & ConnectedSolutions)
Massachusetts offers a dual incentive structure for solar-plus-storage systems.
*   **SMART Programme**: The Solar Massachusetts Renewable Target (SMART) programme provides a performance-based incentive *adder* for solar systems paired with qualifying storage. It is an ongoing volumetric payment, not an upfront rebate.
*   **ConnectedSolutions**: A performance-based Virtual Power Plant (VPP) program. Utilities (like Eversource and National Grid) pay a targeted incentive (e.g., **$275 per kW**) based on the battery's average discharge performance during called summer grid events.
*   **Status**: Active. Check [Mass Save](https://www.masssave.com/residential) for ConnectedSolutions and the [MA DOER](https://www.mass.gov) for SMART block capacity.

### 4. Hawaii (Smart DER / BYOD Plus)
*   **Program**: [Smart DER / BYOD Plus](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-choice)
*   **Incentive**: Upfront cash incentive based on committed kW and ongoing monthly credits.
*   **Note**: The legacy Battery Bonus program is closed to new applicants.

## Part 3: Rural Incentives (REAP)

The [USDA REAP](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/energy-programs/rural-energy-america-program-renewable-energy-systems-energy-efficiency-improvement-guaranteed-loans) provides assistance to agricultural producers and rural small businesses.
*   **Status (2026):** REAP is currently accepting applications for guaranteed loans. However, the grant portion of the programme is not accepting new applications and further grant awards are paused.
*   **Eligibility:** Rural small businesses and agricultural producers (not residential homeowners).

## Application Tips

When applying for state or utility rebates, meticulous attention to detail is necessary:

### 1. Match the Names Perfectly
The name on the Utility Bill, the Solar Contract, and the Rebate Application must match precisely to avoid processing delays.

### 2. Record Serial Numbers
Verify the actual serial number on the installed unit matches your application.

### 3. Monitor Communications
Program Administrators communicate via email. Respond promptly to any clarification requests to ensure your application remains active.

## Engineering Reality

US state battery rebate programmes are dynamic and frequently mischaracterised in national publications. Understanding the operational mechanics of state rebates prevents both missed opportunities and incorrect financial projections.

**State rebates are funded programmes, not entitlements.** Unlike the federal ITC — which was a statutory tax credit — state battery rebate programmes are funded from specific budget allocations that deplete over time. New York's NY-Sun programme and California's SGIP operate with block or tranche-based budget limits. When the budget for a given period is exhausted, no new applications are accepted or projects are waitlisted. A homeowner who receives a verbal quote that includes a specific state rebate has no guarantee of receiving that rebate unless an application has been submitted and confirmed by the Program Administrator.

**Historical Basis Reduction for Carry-Forward Claims.** For eligible installations placed in service before 1 January 2026, a rebate from a utility or state administrator (like SGIP) had to be deducted from the homeowner's cost basis before calculating the Section 25D federal ITC. This does not apply to new 2026 purchases, as the federal credit has expired, but it remains critical for homeowners filing carry-forward claims on their 2026 tax returns for 2025 purchases.

**Property tax exemption for solar and battery installations varies by state and has important scope limitations.** Many states exempt solar installation value from property tax assessments. However, this exemption does not apply uniformly to battery storage: some states (New York, Massachusetts) have explicit property tax exemptions for both solar and battery storage; others (Texas, Georgia) have exemptions that apply only to solar generation equipment. Claiming a property tax exemption for a standalone battery installation in a jurisdiction where the exemption applies only to solar generation equipment risks a property tax reassessment. Verify the specific scope of your state's exemption with the local assessor's office.

## When This Approach Breaks Down

State rebate guidance requires specific verification in several market contexts where standard national guidance is inaccurate.

**Utility service territory vs. state boundary misalignment.** Some state rebate programmes are tied to specific utilities' service territories rather than state boundaries. A homeowner in a rural area of New York served by National Fuel Gas may not have access to Con Edison or NYSEG utility rebates that are available to most other New York State homeowners. Confirming which electric utility services the property — and what specific rebate programmes that utility offers — is a prerequisite to applying state rebate guidance.

**Net metering policy interaction with battery economics.** In states that have modified or eliminated net metering (California NEM 3.0, Nevada, Arkansas) the economics of solar+battery systems differ fundamentally from states with standard net metering. State rebate programmes in these markets were typically designed under different economic assumptions. Battery sizing guidance developed under retail net metering overestimates battery value in net billing (NEM 3.0) states for solar-only comparison purposes.

**Rental property rebate restrictions.** Most state residential rebate programmes restrict eligibility to owner-occupied primary residences. A landlord who installs a battery in a rental property to benefit tenants may be ineligible for the residential rebate, even if the utility-facing meter is in the landlord's name. Some states have separate commercial or multifamily rebate programmes — confirm eligibility category before applying.

## Engineering Recommendation

US state battery rebates represent genuine and significant financial contributions to installation cost, but only for homeowners who actively pursue the applications before and during installation. Passive assumption of rebate entitlement is the most common reason for missed incentive capture.

**The correct US state rebate pursuit process:**
1. Identify your electric utility service territory (your utility bill shows the company name — confirm this is the operating utility, not a billing aggregator).
2. Search the utility's website for "battery rebate," "energy storage incentive," "ConnectedSolutions," and "VPP" — terminology varies by utility.
3. Search your state's energy office website for "residential battery storage rebate" — confirm the current programme status (active, pending, exhausted) and the required application timing (pre-installation vs. post-installation).
4. Check the Database of State Incentives for Renewables and Efficiency (DSIRE.org) for a comprehensive state-by-state listing.

**The key decision trigger** for state rebate action is the specific programme's application timing requirement. If the programme requires a pre-installation application (like California SGIP), confirm and receive written approval before booking an installation date.
