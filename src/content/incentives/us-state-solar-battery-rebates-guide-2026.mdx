The sticker price of a home battery system—often $15,000 or more—is significant, but state and utility incentives can reduce the final cost.

**Update (August 2026):** The federal Section 25D residential ITC was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. State rebates, utility incentives, and VPP payments remain available and are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

This guide aggregates solar battery incentives available across the United States. We will highlight state-level programs, performance payments, and how to evaluate their impact.

---

## Part 1: The Foundation (Previously the Federal ITC)

The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (30% of the total installed cost) was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. **No federal residential ITC is available for new 2026 battery purchases.** State rebates, utility programmes, and VPP payments are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

If you installed before 1 January 2026 and have unused credit, you may carry that forward. Review the [US Federal ITC status guide](/incentives/us-federal-solar-battery-tax-credit-itc) for carry-forward rules.

### For Reference: How the Expired ITC Worked (Pre-2026)
The credit was applied on IRS Form 5695 and reduced federal income tax liability dollar-for-dollar. It was non-refundable, so unused credit carried forward. It applied to the net cost after any utility rebates reduced the ITC basis. This is now historical guidance only — it does not apply to 2026 purchases.

### Direct Pay (For Non-Profits)
If you are a church, school, or non-profit, you don’t pay taxes, so a “tax credit” is useless.
**Good News**: The IRA introduced “Direct Pay.” The IRS will actually write you a check for 30% of the cost. This is a game-changer for community centers. Note: Direct Pay applies to commercial and non-profit credits (Section 48/48E) — it does not apply to the expired Section 25D residential homeowner credit.

## Part 2: States with Active Incentive Programs

Several states offer cash rebates or performance payments for energy storage.

### 1. California (SGIP)
*   **Program**: [Self-Generation Incentive Program (CPUC)](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip)
*   **Incentive**: Up to **$1,100/kWh** under the current Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, subject to available funding.
*   **Status**: Check current budget availability before assuming a tier is open.
*   **Note**: The current Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget is distinct from other categories (e.g., Equity Resiliency, Small Residential Storage) that the CPUC identified as available only through 2025. Equity tiers are subject to specific eligibility requirements and potential waitlists.

### 2. Hawaii (Smart DER / BYOD Plus)
*   **Program**: [Smart DER / BYOD Plus](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-choice)
*   **Incentive**: Upfront cash incentive based on committed kW and ongoing monthly credits.
*   **Note**: The legacy Battery Bonus program is closed to new applicants.

### 3. Maryland (FY26 Energy Storage Grant)
*   **Program**: [FY26 Energy Storage Grant Program](https://energy.maryland.gov/Pages/Energy-Storage-Grant-Program.aspx)
*   **Status**: Closed for FY26. Applications were accepted until June 5, 2026 or until funding was exhausted.
*   **Note**: The previous Maryland Energy Storage Income Tax Credit ended in 2024.

### 4. Massachusetts (ConnectedSolutions)
*   **Program**: [Mass Save ConnectedSolutions](https://www.masssave.com/residential/rebates-offers-services/battery-storage-and-evs/batteries/)
*   **Incentive**: Performance-based VPP payments.
*   **Value**: **$275 per kW** of average summer contribution.
*   **Impact**: Payments are based on measured performance during dispatch events.

## Part 3: Rural Incentives (REAP)

The [USDA REAP](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/energy-programs/rural-energy-america-program-renewable-energy-systems-energy-efficiency-improvement-guaranteed-loans) provides assistance to agricultural producers and rural small businesses.
*   **Status (2026):** REAP is currently accepting applications for guaranteed loans. However, the grant portion of the programme is not accepting new applications and further grant awards are paused.
*   **Historical Grants:** Historically, the programme offered grants for up to 50% of total project cost.
*   **Eligibility:** Rural small businesses and agricultural producers.

## Application Tips

When applying for state or utility rebates, meticulous attention to detail is necessary:

### 1. Match the Names Perfectly
The name on the Utility Bill, the Solar Contract, and the Rebate Application must match precisely to avoid processing delays.

### 2. Record Serial Numbers
Verify the actual serial number on the installed unit matches your application.

### 3. Monitor Communications
Program Administrators communicate via email. Respond promptly to any clarification requests to ensure your application remains active.

## Engineering Reality

US state battery rebate programmes are dynamic and frequently mischaracterised in national publications. Understanding the operational mechanics of state rebates prevents both missed opportunities and incorrect financial projections.

**State rebates are funded programmes, not entitlements.** Unlike the federal ITC — which is a statutory tax credit available to all eligible filers — state battery rebate programmes are funded from specific budget allocations that deplete over time. New York's NY-Sun programme, California's SGIP, and Massachusetts ConnectedSolutions performance programme all operate with annual or tranche-based budget limits. When the budget for a given period is exhausted, no new applications are accepted until the next funding round. A homeowner who receives a verbal quote that includes a specific state rebate has no guarantee of receiving that rebate unless an application has been submitted and confirmed with a reference number.

**Utility rebates layered on top of state programmes reduce the federal ITC basis (Historical rule for 2025 carry-forward filers).** For eligible installations placed in service before 1 January 2026, a rebate from XCEL Energy (Colorado), PEPCO (DC/Maryland), or Pacific Gas & Electric (California) had to be deducted from the homeowner's cost basis before calculating the Section 25D federal ITC. This was IRS-mandated: utility rebates were treated as purchase price reductions, not income. A system that cost $18,000, received a $3,000 utility rebate, and then applied the historical 30% Section 25D federal ITC calculated the credit on $15,000 ($4,500 credit) — not on $18,000 ($5,400 credit). An installer or financial model that does not apply this basis reduction to a carry-forward claim is creating an incorrect financial projection and potentially an IRS overclaim. This does not apply to new 2026 purchases, as the federal credit has expired.

**Property tax exemption for solar and battery installations varies by state and has important scope limitations.** Many states exempt solar installation value from property tax assessments — preventing the home's assessed value from increasing as a result of the solar installation. However, this exemption does not apply uniformly to battery storage: some states (New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts) have explicit property tax exemptions for both solar and battery storage; others (Texas, Georgia) have exemptions that apply only to solar generation equipment and may be construed to exclude standalone battery storage. Claiming a property tax exemption for a standalone battery installation in a jurisdiction where the exemption applies only to solar generation equipment risks a property tax reassessment. Verify the specific scope of your state's exemption with the local assessor's office.

## When This Approach Breaks Down

State rebate guidance requires specific verification in several market contexts where standard national guidance is inaccurate.

**Utility service territory vs. state boundary misalignment.** Some state rebate programmes are tied to specific utilities' service territories rather than state boundaries. A homeowner in a rural area of New York served by National Fuel Gas (primarily a natural gas utility with limited electric service territory) may not have access to Con Edison or NYSEG utility rebates that are available to most other New York State homeowners. Confirming which electric utility services the property — and what specific rebate programmes that utility offers — is a prerequisite to applying state rebate guidance.

**Net metering policy interaction with battery economics.** In states that have modified or eliminated net metering (California NEM 3.0, Nevada, Arkansas) the economics of solar+battery systems differ fundamentally from states with NEM 1.0 or NEM 2.0. State rebate programmes in these markets were typically designed under different economic assumptions. A rebate programme size calibrated to offset a portion of the cost of a system with high NEM 2.0 bill savings may be undersized relative to the reduced savings available under NEM 3.0. Battery sizing guidance developed under NEM 2.0 economics overestimates battery value in NEM 3.0 states for solar-only comparison purposes.

**Rental property rebate restrictions.** Most state residential rebate programmes restrict eligibility to owner-occupied primary residences. A landlord who installs a battery in a rental property to benefit tenants may be ineligible for the residential rebate, even if the utility-facing meter is in the landlord's name. Some states have separate commercial or multifamily rebate programmes — confirm eligibility category before applying.



## Engineering Recommendation

US state battery rebates represent genuine and significant financial contributions to installation cost, but only for homeowners who actively pursue the applications before and during installation. Passive assumption of rebate entitlement is the most common reason for missed incentive capture.

**The correct US state rebate pursuit process:**
1. Identify your electric utility service territory (your utility bill shows the company name — confirm this is the operating utility, not a billing aggregator)
2. Search the utility's website for "battery rebate," "energy storage incentive," and "solar+battery rebate" — all three phrases are used across different utility programmes
3. Search your state's energy office website for "residential battery storage rebate" — confirm the current programme status (active, pending, exhausted) and the required application timing (pre-installation vs. post-installation)
4. Check the Database of State Incentives for Renewables and Efficiency (DSIRE.org) for a comprehensive state-by-state listing — note that DSIRE listings may lag active programme changes by 30–90 days and should be verified against the programme administrator's website

**Stacking priority (for 2026 purchases without federal ITC):**
- Utility rebate and state rebate (if terms permit stacking)
- For eligible 2025 filers with carry-forward credits: confirm ITC basis reduction for utility-paid rebates with a tax professional

**The key decision trigger** for state rebate action is the specific programme's application timing requirement. If the programme requires a pre-installation application, confirm and receive written approval before booking an installation date.
