The sticker price of a home battery system—often $12,000 to $15,000 or more—is significant, but state and utility incentives can reduce the final cost.

**Critical Update (September 2026):** The federal Section 25D residential ITC was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. State rebates, utility incentives, and Virtual Power Plant (VPP) payments remain available and are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

This guide aggregates solar battery incentives available across the United States. We highlight state-level programs, performance payments, and how to evaluate their impact.

---

## Part 1: The Foundation (Post-Federal ITC)

The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit (30% of the qualified cost) was terminated for systems placed in service after 31 December 2025. **No federal residential ITC is available for new 2026 battery purchases.** State rebates, utility programmes, and VPP payments are now the primary route to reducing 2026 battery costs.

If you installed before 1 January 2026 and have unused credit, you may carry that forward. Review the [US Federal ITC status guide](/incentives/us-federal-solar-battery-tax-credit-itc) for carry-forward rules.

## Part 2: States with Active Incentive Programs

Several states offer upfront cash rebates and/or ongoing performance payments for energy storage.

### 1. California (SGIP)
*   **Program**: [Self-Generation Incentive Program (CPUC)](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip)
*   **Incentive**: Up to **$1,100/kWh** under the current Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, subject to available funding and cost caps.
*   **Status**: The CPUC has prioritized remaining residential SGIP funding for the Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, which requires qualifying low-income verification and paired solar-plus-storage. Funding is subject to active availability.

### 2. New York (NYSERDA and VDER)
New York structures its residential storage incentives depending on location and system type.
*   **Residential Energy Storage Incentives**: NYSERDA provides residential storage incentives accessible through participating contractors for qualifying systems. (Source: [NYSERDA Residential Storage](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/Energy-Storage-Program/Home-Energy-Storage/Residential-Energy-Storage-Incentives))
*   **NY-Sun (Long Island)**: Single-family storage incentives for Long Island are specifically governed through the NY-Sun program, separate from the broader state residential storage incentives.
*   **VDER (Value of Distributed Energy Resources)**: For qualifying retail or distributed projects on applicable tariffs, VDER provides a compensation mechanism based on the value of exported power to the grid. It is not a universal homeowner upfront rebate. (Source: [NYSERDA Retail Storage](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/Energy-Storage-Program/Developers-and-Contractors/Residential-and-Retail-Storage-Incentives))

### 3. Massachusetts (SMART & ConnectedSolutions)
Massachusetts offers a dual incentive structure for solar-plus-storage systems.
*   **SMART 3.0 Programme**: The Solar Massachusetts Renewable Target (SMART) programme provides an Energy Storage Multiplier for solar systems paired with qualifying storage. It is an ongoing volumetric payment based on the published SMART 3.0 rates. (Source: [SMART 3.0 Program Details](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/smart-30-program-details))
*   **ConnectedSolutions**: A performance-based Virtual Power Plant (VPP) program. The program offers a targeted incentive of **$275 per kW** based on the battery's average summer contribution during dispatched events, subject to current eligibility and programme terms. (Source: [Mass Save ConnectedSolutions](https://www.masssave.com/residential/rebates-offers-services/battery-storage-and-evs/batteries/))

### 4. Hawaii (Smart DER / BYOD Plus)
*   **Program**: [Smart DER / BYOD Plus](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-choice)
*   **Incentive**: Upfront cash incentive based on committed kW and ongoing monthly credits.
*   **Note**: The legacy Battery Bonus program is closed to new applicants.

## Part 3: Rural Incentives (REAP)

The [USDA REAP](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/energy-programs/rural-energy-america-program-renewable-energy-systems-energy-efficiency-improvement-guaranteed-loans) provides assistance to agricultural producers and rural small businesses.
*   **Status (2026):** REAP is currently accepting applications for guaranteed loans. However, the grant portion of the programme is not accepting new applications and further grant awards are paused.
*   **Eligibility:** Rural small businesses and agricultural producers (not residential homeowners).

## Summary of 2026 Incentive Strategy

In 2026, a home battery purchase should include investigating state and utility rebates.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **No federal residential ITC for 2026 purchases** (Section 25D expired December 2025). Focus entirely on your state and utility programmes.
2.  **Check Discovery Resources**: Review your state's listed programmes on discovery resources like the Database of State Incentives for Renewables and Efficiency (DSIRE). Note that DSIRE must be checked against official programme administrators for current eligibility and funding.
3.  **Check Utility**: Contact your power company and ask about "Demand Response", "VPP", or "BYOD" programs.

Depending on where you live, state incentives can significantly offset the cost of a battery system.

## Engineering Reality

US state battery rebate programmes are dynamic. Understanding the operational mechanics of state rebates prevents incorrect financial projections.

**State rebates are funded programmes, not entitlements.** Unlike the federal ITC — which was a statutory tax credit — state battery rebate programmes are funded from specific budget allocations that deplete over time. California's SGIP operates with tranche-based budget limits. When the budget for a given period is exhausted, no new applications are accepted or projects are waitlisted. A homeowner who receives a verbal quote that includes a specific state rebate has no guarantee of receiving that rebate unless an application has been submitted and confirmed by the Program Administrator.

**Historical Basis Reduction for Carry-Forward Claims.** For eligible installations placed in service before 1 January 2026, public-utility subsidies for purchasing or installing qualified property generally reduce qualified expenses. State incentives are generally not subtracted unless they qualify as a rebate or purchase-price adjustment under federal tax law. The treatment depends on the payment's structure. Carry-forward filers should use current IRS instructions or a qualified tax professional.

## When This Approach Breaks Down

State rebate guidance requires specific verification in several market contexts where standard national guidance is inaccurate.

**Utility service territory vs. state boundary misalignment.** Some state rebate programmes are tied to specific utilities' service territories rather than state boundaries. Confirming which electric utility services the property — and what specific rebate programmes that utility offers — is a prerequisite to applying state rebate guidance.

**Net metering policy interaction with battery economics.** In states that have modified or eliminated net metering, the economics of solar+battery systems differ fundamentally from states with standard net metering.

## Engineering Recommendation

US state battery rebates represent a source of financial contribution to installation cost. Application timing requirements vary by programme; some programmes, including California SGIP, require approval before installation begins. Confirm current programme requirements directly with each programme administrator before booking an installation date.

**The correct US state rebate pursuit process:**
1. Identify your electric utility service territory (your utility bill shows the company name — confirm this is the operating utility, not a billing aggregator).
2. Search the utility's website for "battery rebate," "energy storage incentive," "ConnectedSolutions," and "VPP" — terminology varies by utility.
3. Search your state's energy office website for "residential battery storage rebate" — confirm the current programme status (active, pending, exhausted) and the required application timing (pre-installation vs. post-installation).
4. Use discovery resources like DSIREusa.org, but always confirm actual availability with the administrator.

**The key decision trigger** for state rebate action is the specific programme's application timing requirement. If the programme requires a pre-installation application (like California SGIP), confirm and receive written approval before booking an installation date.
