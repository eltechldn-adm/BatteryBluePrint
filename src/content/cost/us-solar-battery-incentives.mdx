**Update (August 2026):** The US federal Section 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit was terminated for residential property placed in service after 31 December 2025. The credit is unavailable for new 2026 residential battery purchases. See the [dedicated ITC update page](/incentives/us-federal-solar-battery-tax-credit-itc) for details.

An ordinary homeowner buying and owning a battery for personal residential use does not receive a replacement personal 30% credit under Section 48E. Section 48E is a separate business and entity investment credit.

Some states and utilities offer performance payments or upfront rebates, which vary significantly by location and current funding availability.

This guide explains how these programmes work and which states offer dedicated battery funding.

---

## 1. Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

*   **Status (2026):** Section 25D terminated for residential installations placed in service after December 31, 2025.
*   **Value:** **30% of Gross Cost** — applicable only to qualifying installations placed in service before 1 January 2026.
*   **For 2025 filers:** If you installed before 1 January 2026 under Section 25D and had insufficient tax liability, unused residential credit may be applied in future tax years via IRS Form 5695.

### Commercial Credit (Section 48E) for Third-Party Ownership
A qualifying commercial or third-party owner may potentially claim the Section 48E Clean Electricity Investment Credit, depending on taxpayer, ownership, project, equipment, placed-in-service and other statutory requirements.
- If you lease a battery or enter a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA), the third-party owner may claim this credit.
- Any benefit passed to a residential customer through a lease or PPA is contractual — it is not a tax credit claimed directly by the homeowner.

### Key Rules (Historical — for eligible pre-2026 filers)
1.  **Standalone is OK:** You did *not* need to have solar panels. A standalone battery charged from the grid qualified.
2.  **Size Limit:** Must have been at least 3 kWh (which covers basically all home batteries).
3.  **New Equipment:** Could not be used cells.

## 2. State-Specific Incentives

With no federal residential ITC available for 2026 purchases, these state programmes are now the primary source of incentive stacking for new US buyers.

### California: SGIP (Self-Generation Incentive Program)
*   **Source:** [CPUC SGIP](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip)
*   **Status:** Check current budget availability through the [program metrics portal](https://www.selfgenca.com/home/program_metrics) before assuming a tier is open.
*   **Value:** Up to $1,100/kWh under the current Residential Solar and Storage Equity budget, subject to available funding.
*   **Equity / Medical:** Distinguish current Residential Solar and Storage Equity funding from categories identified as available only through 2025 (e.g., Equity Resiliency). Equity tiers are subject to specific eligibility requirements and potential waitlists.

### Maryland: FY26 Energy Storage Grant Program
*   **Source:** [Maryland Energy Administration](https://energy.maryland.gov/Pages/Energy-Storage-Grant-Program.aspx)
*   **Status:** Closed for FY26 (applications were accepted until June 5, 2026 or until funding was exhausted).
*   **Note:** The previous Maryland Energy Storage Income Tax Credit ended in 2024. The successor FY26 grant program has fully allocated its funding at this time.

### Massachusetts: ConnectedSolutions
*   **Source:** [Mass Save](https://www.masssave.com/residential/rebates-offers-services/battery-storage-and-evs/batteries/)
*   **Type:** Performance Payment (Not upfront cash).
*   **Value:** $275 per kW of average summer contribution.
*   **Impact:** Payments are based on measured performance during dispatch events. Eligibility depends on the customer, sponsor and qualifying equipment.

### Hawaii: Smart DER / Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) Plus
*   **Source:** [Hawaiian Electric Customer Choice](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-choice) / [BYOD Plus](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-incentive-programs/bring-your-own-device-plus)
*   **Programs:** Hawaiian Electric currently offers Smart DER and BYOD Plus. (The legacy Battery Bonus program is closed to new applicants).
*   **Value:** Upfront cash incentive based on committed kW and ongoing monthly credits (for BYOD Plus).

## 3. Utility Virtual Power Plants (VPP)

Virtual Power Plants (VPPs) are utility programmes that offer incentives in exchange for permission to discharge your battery to support the grid during periods of peak demand.

*   **Structure:** Programmes vary by utility. Some offer an upfront equipment discount, while others provide ongoing performance payments based on capacity contribution.
*   **Dispatch Events:** The utility controls the battery discharge during defined "events." Event frequency and depth of discharge are governed by the specific programme terms. Programme rules may limit or preclude opt-outs during critical grid strain periods.
*   **Example:** Tesla Electric (Texas) allows Powerwall owners to sell power at wholesale market rates during grid strain events.

## 4. Rural Energy for America Program (REAP)

The [USDA REAP](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/energy-programs/rural-energy-america-program-renewable-energy-systems-energy-efficiency-improvement-guaranteed-loans) provides assistance to agricultural producers and rural small businesses.

*   **Status (2026):** REAP is currently accepting applications for guaranteed loans. However, the grant portion of the programme is not accepting new applications and further grant awards are paused pending revised regulations.
*   **Eligibility:** Rural small businesses and agricultural producers.
*   **Historical Grants:** Historically, the programme offered grants for up to 50% of total project cost. Applications required significant documentation, often necessitating a professional grant writer.

## 5. Regional Considerations

Availability of battery incentives varies by location and depends on current local energy policy and utility structures.

-   **California:** The [CPUC SGIP](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/sgip) programme offers varying incentives, subject to budget caps.
-   **Massachusetts:** The [Mass Save ConnectedSolutions](https://www.masssave.com/residential/rebates-offers-services/battery-storage-and-evs/batteries/) programme offers performance-based payments for battery dispatch.
-   **Hawaii:** High electricity rates combine with [Smart DER/BYOD Plus](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/products-and-services/customer-choice) programmes.
-   **Texas:** Utilities like [Austin Energy](https://austinenergy.com/rates/residential-rates/value-of-solar-rate) offer Value of Solar (VoS) rates, which affect battery economics, though Austin Energy does not provide a direct battery equipment rebate.

## How to Claim Carry-Forward ITC Credits (2025 Filers Only)

This section applies **only to homeowners who installed before 1 January 2026** and have unused Section 25D credit to carry forward. If you installed in 2026, this section does not apply to you.

### Step 1: Confirm Eligibility

You must:
- Have owned (not leased) the solar and/or battery system
- Have had the system installed at your primary or secondary US residence **before 1 January 2026**
- Have unused credit remaining from a prior qualifying tax year

If you installed in 2026, stop here — no Section 25D credit is available.

### Step 2: Complete IRS Form 5695

The carry-forward credit is claimed on **IRS Form 5695** (Residential Energy Credits). You’ll need:
- The total installed cost of the original qualifying system (from your installer invoice)
- The date the system was placed in service (must be before 1 January 2026)
- The amount of credit generated but not yet used

Your tax professional should confirm the correct carry-forward amount.

### Step 3: Apply the Credit to Your Tax Return

The carried-forward credit reduces your federal income tax liability dollar-for-dollar in the tax year it is applied. The carry-forward continues until fully consumed or until any legislative reinstatement changes the rules.

### Step 4: Keep Your Documentation

Retain all receipts, permits, and installer documentation for your records to substantiate your qualified expenditures. Consult IRS guidance on recordkeeping for the required retention period for your specific situation.

## Common Questions (FAQ)

### Can I claim the ITC if I have no federal tax liability? (For eligible pre-2026 property)
The Section 25D credit is non-refundable. If you had no federal tax liability, you could not directly benefit from the credit in that year. Under IRS rules, excess unused residential credit may be applied in future years. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

### Does the ITC apply to battery-only installations (no solar)? (For eligible pre-2026 property)
As of the end of 2025, the Section 25D residential ITC has expired for new residential installations. For property placed in service before 1 January 2026, eligible standalone residential batteries (with a capacity of at least 3 kWh) qualified for the Section 25D credit without any renewable charging source requirement.

### What is the difference between a tax credit and a tax deduction?
A tax credit reduces your tax bill dollar-for-dollar. A tax deduction reduces your taxable income. The historical Section 25D incentive was a non-refundable tax credit.

### Was the ITC extended? What is its current status?
The Section 25D residential credit was terminated effective January 1, 2026 by H.R. 1 (signed July 2025). It is not currently available for new residential battery installations. For the most current information on what incentives remain, see our [US state rebates guide](/incentives/us-state-solar-battery-rebates-guide-2026).

## Engineering Reality

US battery incentive programmes are legally well-established, but the administrative details of claiming them create outcomes that frequently differ from the headline benefit.

**ITC non-refundability (relevant for 2025 carry-forward filers).** The historical Section 25D credit is non-refundable and reduces Federal tax liability. Full utilisation requires sufficient Federal tax liability. Payback calculations that assume Year 1 full credit utilisation overstate the financial benefit for purchasers who did not have sufficient tax liability to absorb the entire credit in the year of installation. Unused residential credit may be applied in future years.

**Battery-only ITC claims (2025 filers, carry-forward).** For eligible installations placed in service before 1 January 2026, standalone residential battery storage (minimum 3 kWh capacity) qualified for the Section 25D credit. Unlike previous iterations of the commercial ITC (Section 48), the residential Section 25D credit as amended by the IRA did not impose a renewable charging source requirement for standalone battery storage technology.

**SGIP funding rounds are exhausted within hours or days of opening.** California's Self-Generation Incentive Program is described as offering $150–$1,000/kWh depending on tier. The Equity Resiliency tier (providing near-full system cost coverage for qualifying households in High Fire Threat Districts) is genuinely available but is significantly oversubscribed relative to annual funding allocations. Budget notifications from CPUC indicate remaining Equity Resiliency funds are typically depleted within days of each funding round opening. A homeowner in an eligible area who has not pre-registered their interest is unlikely to capture this specific tier without a multi-year wait.

**VPP programme terms allow unilateral dispatch changes.** Virtual Power Plant programmes that provide upfront discounts in exchange for dispatch rights are correctly described as offering $2,000–$4,000 in upfront value. However, VPP contract terms typically allow the utility to modify the Annual Dispatch Event limit, season timing, and payment structure with 60–90 days notice. A homeowner who modelled backup resilience around 15 annual dispatch events may find the programme terms allow 40 events in a severe heat wave summer, partially compromising backup availability.

## When This Approach Breaks Down

US incentive stacking is frequently presented as an additive calculation. Several programme limitations make the full stack unavailable in specific circumstances.

**Tax households with complex liability situations (2025 carry-forward filers).** The historical Section 25D credit is non-refundable. Households with complex tax situations should confirm with a tax professional regarding their credit utilisation timeline before counting on full Year 1 absorption.

**Leased systems and the commercial ITC.** For 2026 residential installations, the Section 25D homeowner credit is unavailable. However, homeowners who select a lease or PPA arrangement may see the financing company claim the commercial Investment Tax Credit (Section 48), which remains active. If the lender simultaneously claims a state rebate on the homeowner's behalf, the homeowner may end up with no direct incentive capture while still committed to a 20-year system contract. This is not inherently problematic if the monthly lease payment versus bill reduction calculation is attractive, but the "80% incentive stack" scenario described for REAP-eligible properties requires ownership, not a lease.

**States with income-tested rebates excluding middle-income households.** Some state programmes — particularly Equity-based tiers within SGIP and New York's Empower programme — target low-income households explicitly. Since the Section 25D residential credit is expired for 2026, middle-income homeowners in these states may receive neither a federal credit nor a state supplement, while lower-income neighbours receive state stacking. In markets where the financial case depends on stacking, homeowners above programme income thresholds should model incentives more conservatively.

**Agricultural REAP grant applications (Historical context).** Historically, the 50% REAP grant for rural agricultural properties offered substantial value but required significant administrative complexity. Applications required technical reports from certified energy auditors, site-specific calculations, and multi-year financial projections. With new grant awards currently paused for 2026, rural businesses must rely on the REAP guaranteed loan programme or other incentives rather than modeling a 50% upfront grant reduction.

## Engineering Recommendation

US state and utility incentive programmes collectively represent genuine and substantial financial support for battery investment. A comprehensive incentive stack — combining state rebates, utility BYOD programmes, and VPP income — is achievable for many 2026 purchasers, but requires proactive programme awareness and careful sequence planning.

**Historical Note on the Federal ITC (Pre-2026 installations only):**
- For property placed in service *before* 1 January 2026, homeowners must file IRS Form 5695 correctly in the tax year the system was placed in service. This guidance applies only to the original eligible credit or its carry-forward. It does not apply to a new 2026 installation.
- If your Federal tax liability in 2025 was below the generated ITC credit value, consult a CPA about carry-forward strategy.

**For 2026 programme-specific eligibility verification:**
- SGIP: Check CPUC's SGIP database and your utility's SGIP administrator (PG&E, SCE, or SoCalGas) to confirm current funding tier and waitlist status.
- Massachusetts ConnectedSolutions: Your utility (Eversource, National Grid) administers the programme — contact them directly to confirm your battery qualifies for dispatch participation.
- REAP grant: Contact your local USDA Rural Development state office to confirm eligibility before paying for a technical report.

**The key decision trigger** for 2026 purchases is whether your state offers a programme that meaningfully reduces the gross cost, given the expiration of the federal residential ITC.
