Solid-state batteries are currently under development by several major automotive and battery manufacturers. This guide explains what the technology is, the manufacturer-announced targets for automotive applications, and what those announcements actually mean for homeowners considering battery storage in 2026.

## 1. What "Solid-State" Means

To understand what manufacturers are developing, we look at their published descriptions.

Toyota and Idemitsu Kosan are focusing on sulfide solid electrolytes, which they describe as having high capacity and output, though they noted that establishing mass production requires overcoming a "short life issue." The focus of their joint development is addressing these durability constraints.

QuantumScape describes its technology as a solid-state lithium-metal battery. According to their technology overview, the architecture is designed to replace the conventional polymer separator and liquid electrolyte with a solid ceramic separator, aiming to increase volumetric energy density.

These specific architectures aim to alter the energy density profile of the cell compared to standard liquid electrolytes, but they require distinct manufacturing and engineering solutions. As an editorial inference, different solid electrolyte classes present distinct engineering trade-offs.

## 3. Manufacturer-Announced Targets

Several manufacturers have published specific targets and timelines for their solid-state battery development, focused on automotive integration.

### Toyota and Idemitsu Kosan
Toyota Motor Corporation and Idemitsu Kosan announced a joint venture target for the commercialisation of solid-state batteries. According to Toyota's official newsroom, the companies are targeting automotive commercialisation in the 2027–2028 timeframe. This is expressly a target for automotive applications, not a report of achieved commercial mass production or a residential launch date. The companies have outlined a multi-phase approach, beginning with pilot production before attempting full-scale commercialisation.

### Samsung SDI
According to Samsung SDI's published announcements, the company is targeting mass production of solid-state batteries. This target represents a company target for its automotive and high-performance battery divisions. The company describes this as a development target, not a confirmed production achievement. Initial production efforts are focused on delivering prototype samples to automotive partners for evaluation.

### QuantumScape
QuantumScape, an automotive battery developer, is developing solid-state lithium-metal batteries. In February 2026, QuantumScape inaugurated its "Eagle Line," which the company describes as a pilot production facility intended for sampling, testing, demonstrations, and integration. This is a pilot facility, not a commercial mass-production line. The cells produced on this line are intended for automotive evaluation.

These announcements describe pilot production, sampling, and future targets for the automotive sector. None of these announcements establishes a residential launch date.

## 4. Manufacturing Scale Considerations

The transition from pilot production to commercial scale involves specific engineering challenges that distinguish automotive development timelines from potential residential product availability.

According to Toyota's joint venture announcement, establishing mass production requires developing new equipment and processes to overcome durability issues and scale up material production. We infer that transitioning from these pilot designs to commercial volume presents distinct manufacturing challenges.

The equipment and processes for mass production of solid-state cells are still in the development and scaling phases. Pilot-line demonstrations and automotive sampling activities are earlier-stage milestones in this process.

Because the development focus is on automotive qualification and scale-up, there is no clear timeline for when or whether solid-state technology will be engineered and qualified specifically for residential stationary storage applications, which have different size, certification, and cost requirements than automotive packs.
