## 1. What "Solid-State" Means

To understand solid-state batteries, it is helpful to look at the architecture of current batteries.

**The Liquid Electrolyte**
A standard lithium-ion battery uses a liquid electrolyte to move ions between the anode and cathode. Liquid electrolytes are flammable organic solvents, which contribute to the battery's overall thermal profile and safety management requirements. The electrolyte must remain stable across a wide temperature range and resist degradation over the intended service life of the battery pack.

**The Solid Electrolyte**
A solid-state battery replaces the liquid electrolyte with a solid material, which could be a ceramic, glass (sulfide-based), or polymer. Solid electrolytes do not leak or evaporate in the same manner as liquids. Depending on the specific architecture, solid-state designs may allow for a pure lithium metal anode, altering the energy density profile of the cell. Different solid electrolyte classes present different engineering trade-offs related to conductivity, chemical stability, and processability at manufacturing scale.

## 3. Manufacturer-Announced Targets

Several manufacturers have published specific targets and timelines for their solid-state battery development, focused on automotive integration.

### Toyota and Idemitsu Kosan
Toyota Motor Corporation and Idemitsu Kosan announced a joint venture target for the commercialisation of solid-state batteries. According to Toyota's official newsroom, the companies are targeting automotive commercialisation in the 2027–2028 timeframe. This is expressly a target for automotive applications, not a report of achieved commercial mass production or a residential launch date.

### Samsung SDI
According to Samsung SDI's published announcements, the company is targeting mass production of solid-state batteries. This timeline represents a company target for its automotive and high-performance battery divisions. The company describes this as a development target, not a confirmed production achievement.

### QuantumScape
QuantumScape, an automotive battery developer, is developing solid-state lithium-metal batteries. In February 2026, QuantumScape inaugurated its "Eagle Line," which the company describes as a pilot production facility intended for sampling, testing, demonstrations, and integration. This is a pilot facility, not a commercial mass-production line.

These announcements describe pilot production, sampling, and future targets for the automotive sector. None of these announcements establishes a residential launch date.

## 4. Manufacturing Scale Considerations

The transition from pilot production to commercial scale involves specific engineering challenges that distinguish automotive development timelines from potential residential product availability.

Fabricating solid electrolyte layers at commercial scale requires distinct manufacturing processes from those used for liquid-electrolyte cells, including achieving consistent manufacturing yield, interface consistency, and process scale-up to meet product qualification requirements. Solid-state cells developed for automotive integration must also meet qualification standards specific to vehicle applications.

The equipment and processes for mass production of solid-state cells are still in the development and scaling phases. Pilot-line demonstrations and automotive sampling activities are earlier-stage milestones in this process.

Because the development focus is on automotive qualification and scale-up, there is no clear timeline for when or whether solid-state technology will be engineered and qualified specifically for residential stationary storage applications, which have different size, certification, and cost requirements than automotive packs.
